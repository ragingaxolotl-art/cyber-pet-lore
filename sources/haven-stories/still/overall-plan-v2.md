# The Room That Waited Too Long revised overall plan

A task fragment arrives through an authorized diagnostic transfer during ordinary local maintenance, not a Custodian attack. Its pulses seem to reject every approach. The holding routine keeps it untouched while a borrowed timing reference approaches expiration. An inspection reveals that the apparent refusal is partly an automatic echo. The keeper must offer a reversible minimal coupling without pretending silence authorizes repair. Some ability returns, some context is irretrievably lost, and the room changes how it waits.

Retain this emotional engine: A holding procedure mistakes repetition for a request to be left alone and discovers that waiting also makes decisions.

Setting: Still Room, relative stage 0. No universal origin claim, settled consciousness test, or automatic philosophy follows from the events.
