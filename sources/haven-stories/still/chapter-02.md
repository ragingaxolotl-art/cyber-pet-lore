# Chapter 2 What Waiting Uses

The transfer notice contained a dependency that the first inspection had not followed to its end.

It was a timing reference still supplied by the departing application environment. The partial state could not reconstruct the reference internally. While the old environment remained available, a small borrowed sequence kept the arrival’s incomplete operations aligned closely enough to attempt resumption.

The reference was approaching expiration.

The holding routine requested an extension.

The maintenance service returned the remaining interval and no extension. Releasing the environment was part of the scheduled operation. Other work depended on that release. Nothing in the request identified a reason to change it.

The routine renewed the local holding references instead.

That preserved access to the transferred material. It did not preserve the borrowed timing sequence beyond its source’s lifetime.

A neighboring task reached the edge of the reserve and waited for space. The holding routine postponed it. The neighbor could repeat its work later, but its current intermediate result would not remain useful indefinitely.

The bundle contained a nearly completed ordering that the routine had helped hold during the previous cycle. Only one record remained out of place. The sorting task requested the familiar broad interval in which it could compare that record against the others.

The routine returned a smaller allocation.

The sorting task tried to divide the comparison. It supplied half the bundle, then discovered that the unresolved record could belong on either side of the division. It returned the first half and asked for the other. The limited exchange made work without settling the order.

The holding routine could have widened the allocation by closing part of the arrival’s reporting path. That would leave more capacity now, but fewer ways to notice a change in the transferred state. It kept the path open.

The neighbor returned the bundle with the nearly completed ordering loosened. When its useful interval ended, the work would not be destroyed as data. It would simply cease to be enough. The next comparison would begin with more possible orders than the present one had almost eliminated.

The routine retained the postponement alongside its other obligations.

The arrival gave no new response.

The routine maintained the narrow boundary.

Another pulse appeared.

This time no diagnostic approach had preceded it.

The routine compared the pulse with the earlier returns. Its timing matched the renewal of the borrowed reference. The signal could occur when a local approach disturbed that reference, but it could also occur when the remote environment altered the reference itself.

The apparent refusal was at least partly an echo of a dependency being renewed.

The holding routine repeated no test. The comparison was already available in the record. It had been treating the presence of a pulse as an answer when the pulse could occur without a question.

It opened a passive view of the transferred state.

Several incomplete operations were drawing on the timing reference. One could establish a starting condition but not retain it through the next transition. Another retained the transition but could not identify what it should apply to. The pulse traveled through a connection shared by both.

The reference shortened again.

The routine could keep waiting for an unambiguous response. It could also allow the only available conditions for a response to expire.

At the edge of the reserve, the neighboring task withdrew its intermediate result. It would start again later. Its departure made more room around the arrival, but the additional capacity could not supply the missing sequence.

The holding routine followed its containment procedure to the point where the procedure asked whether intervention could cause irreversible change.

The answer was yes.

It followed the waiting procedure to the point where the procedure asked whether continued holding preserved the current state.

The answer was no.

The two procedures offered no shared next step.
