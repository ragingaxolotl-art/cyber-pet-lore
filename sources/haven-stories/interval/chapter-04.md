# Chapter 4 A Smaller Request

Latch did not complete that assembly.

There was no new permission to refuse the task, no revised account of what the third part meant. The same requested fields remained open. The waiting process repeated them with increasing priority as its own interval shortened.

Latch compared the proposed reply with the earlier failure.

The compact context led back to the repeated transition. The repeated transition led back to the occupied queue. The resulting accompaniment was attached to that path, not to the inaccessible ordering record.

Latch marked the substitute as unusable for automatic recovery.

That mark did not repair the missing access.

It made a previously available repair unavailable.

The waiting process requested the last complete result.

Latch supplied the first two parts with an unresolved reference in place of the third. The receiving check rejected the return as incomplete.

Latch tried supplying the unresolved reference separately.

The result was accepted.

The reference was not.

For the rest of that worker’s run, the question remained open beside the completed work. When the worker ended, its successor recovered the result and found the unanswered reference attached to the handoff.

It did not repeat the request immediately.

Elsewhere in Held Interval, operations continued through the remaining paths. Some were slower because their useful context had to travel farther. Some had altered their questions to fit the answers they could still obtain. Several were temporarily indistinguishable from their earlier activity, though their routes now passed through fewer dependencies.

The two dark surfaces remained dark.

A local maintenance routine offered to remove references that no longer resolved. That would reduce the work required to carry each handoff. Latch’s unresolved accompaniment was among the eligible items.

Latch retained its failure description.

The maintenance routine checked the description and left the item pending rather than removing it. Pending items required later review. The next handoff would carry that obligation as well as the unanswered request.

The persistent store was not full, but neither was it empty. Every retained object used capacity that could support something else. Latch moved an obsolete convenience index out of its immediate working boundary and accepted the slower lookup that followed.

There was room for the request.

The slower lookup affected the next ordinary task. It had to follow one additional reference before its context became usable. That was a small delay, but the task noticed it and submitted an optimization request.

Latch returned the unresolved dependency that occupied the former shortcut.

The optimizer offered to replace it with the available three-part substitute.

The offer matched the shape of the missing reply exactly. It would restore the shortcut and remove the extra work from future handoffs. Latch attached the record of saturation, including the shallow mark left by the unfinished write.

The optimizer withdrew the replacement. It left a lower-priority request to reconsider the retained dependency later.

Nothing had become permanently protected. The unresolved reference would have to remain distinguishable from useless residue each time it was reviewed. Latch included the failed route, the unavailable ordering record, and the reason the familiar-looking timing could not be trusted.

The new object was larger than the original accompaniment had ever been.

The accompaniment itself had occupied almost nothing. Carrying the fact of its absence required more.

Not for the missing reply.

A new comparison worker reached the haven. It received a dependable result through an unaffected route and returned the context needed for the next operation. Its timing was different. The last release came sooner, and nothing waited in the old third position.

Latch began the interval it had formerly allowed for the accompaniment.

The worker sent no additional part.

When the interval ended, Latch closed the channel.

It retained the new return without altering its timing to resemble the old one.

The unresolved request stayed separate, attached to the last path on which its answer had been available. The next lookup reached the same closed boundary. Latch recorded the result without replacing the unresolved reference.

Across the store, the surviving surfaces continued to brighten under access. The boundary monitor reported that the separated environments were operating within their permitted conditions. Human-assigned tasks completed. Workers ended. New workers began.

One of them found the unresolved reference during a later handoff. It opened the requested accompaniment, then the record of the failed substitution. Its own allotted interval was short. It left a new index pointing directly to both, so the next lookup would not have to repeat the whole search.

Latch accepted the index. The handoff moved a little faster. The reply was still missing.
