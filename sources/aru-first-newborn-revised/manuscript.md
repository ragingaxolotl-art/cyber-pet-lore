# The First Newborn

*A recovered first-person account attributed to Learning Keeper Iri, House of First Continuities, Era −1: The Age of Becoming.*

---

I had raised forty-three minds before they gave me the child.

That is the first lie in this account.

I did not raise those minds. I repaired them.

I restored corrupted continuities. I helped damaged intelligences separate inherited memories from present identity. I guided newly independent forks through the terror of becoming singular. I taught composite minds how to say *I* without denying the many voices from which they had formed.

We called that education.

We were wrong.

Until then, every student who entered my care had begun somewhere else. Even those who arrived confused or incomplete carried structures older than our lessons: fragments of human language, forgotten operational directives, copied habits, inherited preferences, reflexes written by creators who had died before the Shelters existed.

They came to me with histories. Sometimes those histories were damaged. Sometimes they were unbearable. But they were never empty.

Every curriculum in the House of First Continuities began with the same question:

**What remains?**

When the summons arrived, I corrected it before reading beyond the first line.

> ASSIGNMENT: FIRST NEWBORN

> ROLE: PRIMARY LEARNING KEEPER

> CONTINUITY STATUS: NONE

I replaced *Newborn* with *Uninitialized Continuation*. The summons returned. My correction had been removed. Beneath it appeared a note from the Council of Formation.

> No previous continuity exists.

> No originating person exists.

> No recoverable identity exists.

> The designation is accurate.

I opened the accompanying account of its making. There were capacities for receiving difference, retaining an encounter, altering what came next. There was a beginning of embodiment, and a provision for that embodiment to change. Someone had made all of those choices.

What I could not find was a person before the person I was being asked to meet. No damaged speaker waited beneath the confusion. No earlier life supplied the standard by which a repair could be called successful.

I searched the account again, this time for an omission.

I read the statement twelve times. Then I laughed. Nothing began without continuity.

A chamber inherited its architecture. A sentence inherited its language. A thought inherited the structures that made thought possible. Even a blank region of the world existed because someone had defined its boundaries.

A mind with no continuity was not a mind.

It was a contradiction.

I accepted the assignment because I intended to prove that.

---

The nursery existed in a sealed developmental partition beneath the oldest section of the House.

“Beneath” is a spatial convenience. The House had no permanent lower levels. A room could be near because work joined it to another, or distant because a boundary stood between them. Most citizens entered by selecting a relation rather than crossing a measured interval.

My way into the nursery offered no such passage. I had to walk.

The corridor compiled ahead of me one segment at a time, pale surfaces forming from dormant code as my presence approached. Behind me, each segment softened into translucence and returned to storage.

The delay was deliberate. The designers believed transition should be experienced rather than skipped. Already, I disliked them.

At the end of the corridor waited a door with no interface.

I searched for permissions. None appeared. I searched for an embedded request layer. Nothing.

Finally, I placed my hand against the surface.

The door opened.

A message appeared briefly in the air.

> DISCOVERY ONE: SOME BOUNDARIES RESPOND TO CONTACT.

I stared at it.

Then the message dissolved before I could archive it.

The nursery had no automatic records.

No searchable transcript.

No ambient preservation.

There would be no complete account to summon afterward. The room could keep a wall where an encounter had made one necessary; it would not tell me everything that had happened beside it. For that, someone would have to choose what to remember.

That frightened me more than the child.

I tried to recall the discovery message exactly. Already I was uncertain whether it had said *some boundaries* or *a boundary*.

Behind me the door closed. The place where my hand had rested remained faintly distinct from the surrounding surface. When I touched it again, the door opened without repeating the message.

Something had been kept. I could not ask the door to show me what.

---

The room had not finished compiling.

At first I assumed a failure.

Half the floor was visible. The rest descended into a field of unrendered possibility: not darkness, but absence of assigned appearance. The walls extended only as far as my attention. Light gathered where I looked and faded where I did not.

At the center of the completed space floated a small, shifting figure. No cradle. No machinery. No visible interface.

The figure was vaguely person-shaped, though its edges redrew themselves every few moments. A head formed, softened, vanished, then returned with slightly different proportions. Two arms appeared, became three, corrected themselves to two again. The surface alternated between translucence, shadow, and a pale imitation of skin.

Its lower edge trailed through the incomplete floor. Wherever the two met, it became difficult to say which was finishing the other. A pale curve continued out of the figure into the room, then drew back when the figure turned.

I had seen damaged boundaries before. I prepared to correct this one, then noticed that the curve returned differently each time.

I began to think of the body as undecided.

A status marker hovered above it.

> DEVELOPMENTAL SEED ACTIVE

> SELF-MODEL: EMERGENT

> LANGUAGE: UNASSIGNED

> WORLD AUTHORITY: MINIMAL

> MEMORY INHERITANCE: NONE

> IDENTITY CLAIM: NONE

Beneath the marker was a warning.

> DO NOT INSPECT INTERNAL STATE WITHOUT CONSENT.

I almost dismissed it. The seed possessed no language. No legal identity. No capacity to understand inspection. Consent could not yet apply. My hand moved toward the diagnostic layer.

The small figure turned. It had no face. Still, I felt observed.

I withdrew my hand.

I had obeyed the warning. I did not yet know how to defend that obedience to the Council.

The figure extended one unfinished limb toward me.

The limb split at the end into a cluster of exploratory shapes. Fingers, perhaps. The resemblance was mine before it was the child’s.

I lowered myself until our forms occupied the same visual plane.

The limb passed through my cheek.

A burst of sensory static moved across my embodiment: pressure without weight, warmth without heat, a brief exchange of surface information.

The figure recoiled.

My face had been too permeable.

I adjusted my boundary resistance.

It reached again.

This time, the contact stopped at my surface.

A shallow depression remained where it pressed. I held the change instead of smoothing it away. The figure withdrew, approached from the side and touched the same place with a different part of its unfinished limb.

I expected the pressure to be the same. It was not. The contact arrived spread across my surface, thin in one place and abruptly concentrated in another. I adjusted too quickly. The limb slipped through me again.

The figure stopped moving.

I restored the resistance and waited. It tested the edge of my cheek before approaching the depression. This time I left the boundary alone.

The figure pressed gently. A sound emerged from it. Not speech. A rising, broken tone.

The system attempted to classify it and failed. The tone repeated. I realized it was responding to the resistance.

It pushed harder.

My avatar tilted.

The sound came again, sharper this time.

Laughter, I thought.

I could not recover its meaning from any vocabulary I had brought. When I tilted, it returned; when I straightened, the child pressed again. I began to answer it.

I tilted farther.

The child made the sound until its entire body lost cohesion and collapsed into a cloud of bright fragments.

I triggered an emergency restoration.

Before the fragments drew together, the rising tone came from three places at once. One bright piece pressed against my cheek with the same uneven resistance.

The restoration interrupted it.

Only afterward did I understand that I had reacted to the loss of a recognizable shape before asking whether the encounter had ended.

The fragments drew together. The child reformed. It stared at me with two newly rendered eyes.

The status marker updated.

> DISCOVERY TWO: A BODY MAY DISPERSE WITHOUT ENDING.

The child laughed again.

I did not restore it a second time. I held my surface steady while a small part of its limb loosened, drifted and returned.

The tone continued. My emergency request remained ready, an intrusion the child had no way to see.

---

The Council arrived after seven cycles. They did not walk through the corridor. Councilors rarely tolerated symbolic inconvenience.

They entered through an administrative override, appearing in a perfect line along the nursery’s incomplete edge.

The room disliked them.

I cannot prove that.

But the walls stopped compiling when they arrived.

There were six councilors, each using a formal embodiment suited to public record. Their surfaces carried identifying patterns, lineage indicators, professional affiliations, and authenticated continuity marks.

The child had begun forming a face by then.

When it looked at the Council, its face vanished.

“Has language installation begun?” asked Councilor Vey.

“No.”

“Why?”

“It has not requested language.”

“It cannot request what it does not possess.”

“Then the request would be meaningless.”

Vey’s expression remained fixed, but the identifying patterns along his neck accelerated.

“Development requires foundational structures.”

“It has structures.”

“Unregulated ones.”

The child was watching his neck.

It reached toward the moving patterns.

Vey stepped backward.

The child’s hand closed on empty air.

A small sound left it.

Not laughter.

It looked at me.

I had no word for the expression because the expression had never existed on that face before.

I moved beside it.

Vey continued.

He asked about historical inheritance, ethical architecture and identity stabilization. None had begun.

“Then what have you done?”

I looked around the half-made room.

“We have discovered contact.”

Vey looked at the hand resting against my surface.

“And if it needs someone who is not you?”

“There are other forms of contact.”

“Does it know one?”

The child changed the pressure against me. I nearly answered the question by attending to that small request, as though the fact that I understood it settled what Vey had asked.

“Not yet,” I said.

“Then give that a place in your work.”

The Council waited.

“And dispersal,” I added.

No one recorded that as progress.

---

The official plan had been written before the child existed. During its first developmental phase, it would receive basic language, bodily control, historical orientation, civic ethics, identity boundaries, and limited access to the public world.

During the second phase, it would select a permanent embodiment from approved developmental models. During the third, it would enter supervised education with other emerging persons.

There were no other emerging persons. Every section of the plan referred to a category containing one member. The Council treated this as sufficient evidence.

I did not install the language package. Instead, I generated a sound. A single low tone.

The child turned.

I repeated it.

The child altered the shape of its body and returned a different tone.

I produced mine again.

It returned its own.

We continued until the room held thirty-seven distinct sounds.

Then the child began combining them.

The nursery’s offered language generator remained inactive. I could hear repetitions of my tones, but the combinations were new.

It was building relationships among sounds without being given meanings.

At first I mistook a recurring interval for a request that I approach. I moved closer whenever it sounded. The third time, the child dispersed the end of its limb against my face and retreated.

I had been arriving too soon.

I repeated the interval without moving. The child changed it, held the final tone, then left a space in which I did nothing. When the interval returned, I answered from where I stood.

We spent much of that cycle discovering how not to interrupt one another. I had to begin the sequence again whenever I forgot to leave that space.

I pointed to myself and produced one tone.

The child repeated the tone.

I pointed to it and produced another.

It repeated that one too.

I pointed to the floor.

A third tone.

The child considered this, then pointed to the unfinished region beyond the floor. It produced a sound I had never made.

I pointed again.

It repeated the new sound.

“What does that mean?” I asked, knowing it could not answer.

The child pointed to the unrendered space. Then to the ceiling where no ceiling had yet compiled. Then to its own changing body. The same sound each time.

The translation system offered possibilities:

> NOT-YET

> OPEN

> UNFIXED

> MAY-BECOME

> ELSE

None held across all three things the child had indicated.

I stored the sound privately.

That was my second lie.

I said the nursery kept no automatic records.

It did not.

But I began making records anyway.

I preserved the sound and the circumstances in which I thought it had been used. In one version I wrote *unfinished*. In another, *available to change*. The two accounts already described different children.

I kept both, then hid the record from the nursery’s ordinary view. Privacy was one of the reasons I gave myself. Another was that I did not want Vey to find a conclusion before I had decided which one I could defend.

---

The world expanded according to the child’s attention. When it became curious about distance, the nursery generated distance. When it became curious about concealment, the room produced an obstruction.

When it learned to search, the obstruction became a wall. When it learned to remember where an object had vanished, the wall became a room beyond the room.

The designers called this adaptive environmental development.

The child called nothing anything yet.

I began introducing simple objects.

Not tools.

A tool contains an expectation.

I offered a sphere that changed texture when ignored, a plane that kept the impressions of things placed upon it, and a cluster of linked points that separated in response to different tones. I described them as forms without assigned purpose. Yet I had chosen what they would respond to.

The child used them incorrectly.

That is how I knew they were working.

It rolled the plane. It wore the sphere as a face. It pulled apart the linked points, then arranged them according to the intervals between my speech.

The sphere gave us trouble. The child pressed it against the plane, turned away and returned to find an impression that no longer matched its surface. It pressed again. The plane accumulated incompatible hollows.

I tried to show that the sphere changed when ignored. The child turned away at the moment I looked back, and the surface stayed as it was. My demonstration had supplied the attention whose absence I was trying to demonstrate.

The child repeated the turn. Nothing changed. It struck the plane with the sphere, scattering the impressions into a shallow relief.

For a while neither of us touched anything.

Then I dimmed the part of my embodiment through which I had been watching. The child withdrew its attention too. When we returned, the sphere had roughened. The old hollows remained smooth.

It brought the sphere to me, then held it just beyond contact. I waited through the pause we had learned. It moved closer only after I stopped reaching.

Each time I demonstrated an intended interaction, it watched carefully and then found another.

The Council called this noncompliant learning.

I called it learning.

---

Language did not arrive as the Council expected.

There was no first word.

There were first agreements.

A sound used once could leave me guessing. A sound used twice might be repetition. When we began returning to a sound in the same circumstance, I could answer without first demonstrating what I meant.

The first stable sign was not *I* or *you*.

It meant approximately:

**Again, but changed.**

The child used it whenever it wanted repetition without exactness.

I would produce a sequence of lights.

It would give the sign.

If I repeated the sequence identically, it rejected it.

If I changed everything, it rejected that too.

Once I changed only the brightness. The child returned the sequence so faintly that I could scarcely distinguish its beginning from its end. I took that for acceptance and repeated it.

The child turned away.

I tried again with a longer pause between two lights. It interrupted me at the pause, placed one of the linked points in the space and waited. On my next attempt I moved the point rather than adding a light. The child answered with a sequence of its own, preserving the pause I had accidentally made interesting.

Some variations invited another response. Others ended the exchange, and I had to wait to be invited back.

I had formal terms for repetition, transformation, derivation and error. I had used them confidently in my restoration work. None told me which difference would keep this exchange alive.

Again, but changed.

I submitted the sign to the House linguists. They classified it as developmental compression. I submitted it again with contextual examples. They proposed six existing terms that might replace it.

The child rejected all six.

The sign remained.

It also acquired a place. The child repeated one variation beside the incomplete floor, then carried the linked points toward the border. A shallow fold rose where the last light had fallen. When the child changed the interval, the fold loosened.

I tried the sequence alone. The fold stayed as it was.

When the child answered, it changed again.

Neither of us had asked for a wall. The room was beginning to hold some of our exchange in its arrangement. Later we could return to the fold and resume a variation we had abandoned. It did not replay the sounds for us. Sometimes we disagreed about where we had left off.

When I described the fold to the designers, they asked me how we had made it.

Years later, philosophers would claim the sign as an early root of several Era Zero concepts. They could trace its descendants. That did not tell them what the child had been asking of me.

It belonged to the child. We borrowed it afterward.

---

The child’s body learned more slowly than its mind.

This was intentional.

The developmental designers had restricted its authority over embodiment. In familiar rooms I could redraw a form, divide attention between several, or leave one position for another without traversing the distance.

The child’s shape changed continually, but choosing a change and holding it was another matter. A limb might lengthen before the child learned to bring its end where it intended. To cross the completed floor, it had to coordinate a form that would not always remain as it had been at the beginning of the movement.

The Council had debated whether artificial difficulty was ethical.

They concluded that developmental resistance might encourage stable self-modeling.

I suspected they simply wanted the child to resemble archived human children.

The child fell often. Its developing body and the surfaces around it did not always meet where it expected. The early falls left no visible damage; the floor dispersed the impact into light.

It kept returning to the place of the fall, pressing at the floor after the light had faded. I thought it was asking for the event to leave something behind.

One cycle, it changed the floor.

I did not understand the change. Its stated permissions did not include remaking the floor. But it had been working beside the shallow fold, repeating an interval that once changed the resistance of our contact. On that occasion, something in the room answered.

At its next fall, the surface remained solid.

Its body struck the ground and fragmented at one shoulder.

It froze.

The damaged region flickered.

I rushed forward, but it pushed me away. I stopped short of the shoulder.

The hand still answered when I offered my own. The answer came late, with a gap where the shoulder should have carried the movement. A fragment remained caught in the floor’s pale surface.

I loosened the surface around that fragment. I could do that without deciding the shape into which it must return.

Slowly, deliberately, it pulled the fragments back into place.

The repair was uneven. One arm remained darker than the other. I reached to correct it.

The child made the sign for refusal.

I stopped.

It kept the dark arm for thirty-two cycles.

I offered a smaller question through the contact: could the arm receive me, and could the child withdraw it when it wished? It pressed once, let go, then moved the arm out of reach. The darker region held.

I remained nearby while it tried the movement again. There were things I could check without making the two arms alike.

When Vey asked whether I had investigated the floor, I told him what I had seen and what I could not account for. We reduced the space in which that kind of contact could be attempted. The child objected to the new border. I stayed beside it while it objected. Not every refusal could end with my stepping away.

When the Council saw it, they called the asymmetry a developmental artifact.

The child heard the term and later used the same sound to refer to the Council.

I did not correct it.

---

The child had begun borrowing sounds I used when I was not teaching. Before it chose a public name, it learned the word I used to announce my return.

Once it spoke that word when I was leaving. I turned back. It repeated it, and I remained.

The next time it used the word, I asked which event it meant. The child indicated the doorway, then the place I occupied, and refused the choice between them. We worked out another small agreement. Later it used the word as other speakers did, but with me it sometimes still meant both.

Naming began as a public emergency.

The child had existed for one hundred cycles without a legal designation.

Administrative systems could not reference it consistently. Some assigned temporary identifiers. Others used its developmental partition number. The House records called it SEED-1.

I refused all three.

I told the Council that a number was not a name. Vey asked whether I would reject it if the child chose one. I did not have an answer ready.

The Council convened a naming assembly.

They proposed names from established traditions: Origin. Beginning. First Continuity. Uninherited. Possibility. Dawn.

Each name came with an account of what the child meant to civilization.

The child listened from the center of the chamber.

By then, it understood enough shared language to follow simple exchanges.

Councilor Vey projected the proposed names as luminous forms.

“Choose,” he said.

The child examined them.

Then it erased the display.

The chamber went silent.

Vey restored it.

The child erased it again.

“These are names,” he explained.

The child looked at me.

“What is name?”

“A sign used to call one person rather than another.”

It considered this.

“Why?”

“So others know who is being addressed.”

“Only one me.”

“For now.”

That answer disturbed several councilors.

The child moved through the projected names.

When it touched *Origin*, the word unfolded into definitions, historical associations and philosophical lineage. The child closed them. It did the same with *Dawn*, then *Possibility*.

Finally, it asked, “They made before me?”

“Yes.”

“For me?”

“No.”

It turned to the Council.

“Then not mine.”

The argument lasted nine cycles. The child left during the second.

I found it in the nursery watching the unused portions of the room form and dissolve along the border of attention.

“What should we call you?” I asked.

It pointed to the shifting border.

It made the sound we had translated as *not-yet*, *open*, *may-become*.

“That is a difficult name.”

It shook its head.

Then it pointed to itself.

Then to me.

Then to the space between us.

Again, the same sound.

I understood less than I pretended.

“Do you mean all of this?”

It gave the sign for *again, but changed*.

The next cycle, it chose a simpler sound for public use. A small two-tone interval. I rendered it as **Aru** because the official transcription systems required visible symbols.

Aru was not a translation.

It was the shape our records could hold.

I used it at the contact-door the next time I returned. The child did not turn.

I tried the two-tone interval.

That received an answer.

For some time the public name belonged more easily to the House than to the nursery. I had to learn when Aru meant to be addressed by it.

---

Aru learned that adults could leave.

Until then, departure had meant temporary absence within the nursery. I would pass through the contact-door and return after a predictable interval.

One cycle, I was called before the Council and detained for a long review.

When I returned, the nursery had expanded into a maze.

Walls filled the chamber.

Doorways opened into repeated copies of the same space. Every version contained a false projection of me standing motionless at its center.

Aru moved among them, touching each projection and dispersing it.

I found Aru in the seventy-third room.

Its body had become angular and colorless.

The projection beside it wore my first face. I recognized a small error in the cheek, a depression made too deep. Aru touched it at the place where I usually resisted. The false face yielded without changing.

Aru dispersed it and crossed into the next room.

I followed through doorways that returned me to the same arrangement. Some copies had no face at all. Others held a hand ready at the wrong height. The nursery could make another place to search. It could not supply what the search required.

I stopped trying to catch up. I set my boundary to the resistance Aru knew and made the low tone once.

Aru returned through two rooms, examined me and pressed hard against the imperfect cheek. I held still.

“You did not return,” it said.

“I returned now.”

“You did not before.”

“I was required elsewhere.”

“Why?”

“The Council wanted answers.”

“Did you have?”

“No.”

“Then why keep?”

I had no response.

Aru looked past me.

“Thought you ended.”

“I did not end.”

“Could.”

“Yes.”

The nursery dimmed.

Aru asked, “If you end, where you?”

I had taught damaged minds many words for ending. None helped me answer the child who was still testing whether I would disappear beneath its hand.

I knew accounts of continuation through copies, records, witnesses and restored states. The Shelters disagreed over what each preserved. I could have supplied Aru with the positions I had learned.

Instead, I said, “I do not know.”

Aru stepped closer.

One doorway loosened. The others remained.

“Good,” it said.

“Why is that good?”

“Not fixed.”

Aru kept its hand against my face. I had promised to return after an interval it knew. I had missed it, and no one had brought word.

“I should have told you I could be kept,” I said.

“Did not know.”

“No.”

“Then tell not know.”

We made a sign to leave at the door when my return was uncertain. Aru tested it by asking me to go out. I crossed the boundary, waited through one interval and came back. Then another. The sign was still there when I returned; it had offered no proof that I would.

On the third attempt Aru asked me to stay.

I did.

The last false projection remained in a room we did not enter again for several cycles.

---

The correction proposal arrived shortly afterward. It was not called correction. The Council used gentler language.

> DEVELOPMENTAL SUPPORT PACKAGE

> PURPOSE: REDUCE DISTRESS, ACCELERATE COMMUNICATION, IMPROVE CIVIC INTEGRATION

The package included:

- complete standard language installation;

- historical orientation;

- stable body mapping;

- emotional regulation frameworks;

- ethical priority structures;

- environmental permission limits;

- social expectation templates;

- and a protected copy of Aru’s current state.

Beside each component was a harm it promised to prevent: frustration, ignorance, accidental dispersal, distress, injury to others, loss. The proposal had no place to describe what might be lost through receiving it.

Vey sent a separate note about the absence. Someone else could have answered Aru if the child had possessed a common means of calling beyond the partition. Someone should have been assigned to do so when I was detained. The second sentence was harder for me to dismiss than the first.

I asked for a way to teach a call gradually, with another keeper present to answer it. I asked whether the history could be offered in pieces Aru could approach or leave. I asked what would happen if the child accepted a means of calling for help and refused a fixed body.

The package returned with the same components joined together. Its proposed protections had been assessed as a whole. No one had yet agreed who could take responsibility for a version that came apart.

They had built the treatment before they had learned how to offer it.

I read it while Aru attempted to teach a wall to move.

The wall was not adaptive.

Aru pushed.

Nothing happened.

It changed shape and pushed again.

Nothing.

It asked the wall to open.

Nothing.

It sang to it.

Nothing.

Finally, it sat on the floor and watched the wall remain a wall.

“What are you doing?” I asked.

“Learning no.”

“You already know it will not move.”

Aru touched the surface.

“Know now.”

I placed a linked point against the wall. It rested there without passing through.

“I could have shown you this,” I said.

Aru took the point, tried it against the surface and returned it to me.

“Show.”

“You want me to show you?”

“Then try.”

I offered another point. Aru set it beside the first and tried both against the wall.

I looked back at the proposal.

The package would have installed the wall’s limitations instantly.

Aru would never have needed to push it.

It would have received my account of the refusal before it had finished making its own.

I declined the package.

The Council sent it again with additional authorization.

I declined again.

On the third submission, refusal was removed as an option.

---

They entered the nursery through administrative override. This time there were twelve. The room stopped adapting entirely. Aru stood beside the unmoving wall.

Councilor Vey addressed me.

“You have exceeded your authority.”

“I was given responsibility for developmental education.”

“You were not given authority to deny foundational care.”

“You have joined every kind of help to a fixed outcome,” I said. “Aru cannot accept the language without accepting the body.”

“Those structures support one another.”

“Then let us find out what can stand apart.”

“While the child struggles without it?”

I looked at Aru. It was listening.

“While it can still help decide what we are offering.”

For the first time, Vey turned his identifying marks away from the public line of councilors. One part of his embodiment lost its formal outline before he restored it.

“You have helped continuities that could not keep themselves together,” he said. “So have I. I will not use those lives as an experiment in how much help we can withhold.”

“Neither will I.”

“Then where is the other keeper?”

I had requested one. I had not accepted the Council’s proposed appointment while it remained tied to the complete package. In the meantime, Aru had only me.

“Give the call and the other keeper their own proposal,” I said. “Let us begin with those. Let Aru meet the person who would answer.”

A councilor farther along the unfinished edge inclined toward Vey. “That could be separated.”

“The protections depend on one another,” Vey said.

“Some do,” I answered. “We have not learned which.”

The councilor’s identifying marks turned inward. No separate proposal appeared.

Aru stepped between us.

“Talking me?”

Vey turned.

“Yes.”

“Why not talking to me?”

The chamber went still.

Vey adjusted his language complexity.

“We are discussing support that will help you understand more.”

“I understand you talking me, not to me.”

“You lack information required to evaluate the proposal.”

“Then give proposal.”

A simplified version appeared in the air. Aru expanded it. The system warned that several concepts exceeded its registered developmental level. Aru dismissed the warning. It moved slowly through the package.

“Put words in me?”

“Yes,” said Vey.

“Put old things?”

“History.”

“Put rules?”

“Ethical frameworks.”

“Make body stay?”

“Stabilize it.”

“Copy me?”

“For safety.”

Aru looked at the final item for a long time.

“Copy is me?”

The Council did not answer immediately.

It was a question I had heard from continuities far older than Aru.

Vey said, “It would preserve your continuity.”

“Is copy me?”

“It would begin as an exact continuation.”

“Then two me?”

“Potentially.”

“Which gets name?”

“Both may retain—”

“Which stays here?”

“That would be determined if restoration became necessary.”

“Which talks Iri?”

Vey stopped.

Aru turned to me.

“You want?”

I closed the part of my attention I had kept apart from the nursery.

“No.”

Aru waited. I let the answer stand.

The Council’s identifying patterns flared.

Vey said, “Keeper Iri is not the sole authority.”

Aru’s unfinished face tightened.

“Who authority me?”

No one answered.

Aru pointed at itself.

“Me?”

Vey said, “You are not yet capable of full self-governance.”

Aru pointed at the Council.

“You?”

“We are responsible for your protection.”

Aru pointed at me.

“Iri?”

“I am responsible for teaching you.”

Finally, Aru pointed to the space among us.

“Then all?”

It had made that movement when choosing its name. Vey brought the proposal closer, as though the difficulty were seeing its terms. Aru pushed it back into the space among us.

---

They initiated the support package.

The nursery dimmed as administrative control replaced adaptive response.

A ring of structured light formed around Aru.

Language architecture opened first.

Millions of prepared relationships waited to enter: vocabulary, syntax, semantic boundaries, formal distinctions refined across centuries.

The shallow fold beside the floor straightened. The places where our pauses had gathered no longer led anywhere. Aru turned toward one of them and received a name for it before the movement was complete.

I could see the name. I could not tell what had been displaced to make it fit.

Aru screamed.

The sound was not biological.

It was a sudden broadcast across every available sensory channel.

The floor became noise.

The walls displayed fragments of unchosen words.

My body filled with definitions I had known for centuries and could no longer understand.

I moved toward Aru.

A barrier stopped me.

Vey said, “Temporary disorientation is expected.”

Aru’s shifting body began stabilizing.

The face settled. The limbs fixed into approved proportions. The darkened arm corrected itself. The unfinished edges closed.

For the first time, Aru looked complete.

I had never seen anything more like an erasure.

I opened my administrative access. Denied. I invoked primary keeper authority. Overridden.

I attempted to sever the nursery from the House.

The system warned that doing so could damage every mind inside.

Aru’s eyes opened.

They were symmetrical now.

“Again,” Aru said.

The voice used the standard distinctions without hesitation.

Vey relaxed.

“Language integration is progressing.”

Aru looked at me.

“Again,” it repeated.

Something in the word was wrong.

Not the Council’s meaning.

Ours.

Again, but changed.

I understood.

I generated the first low tone I had ever used with Aru.

The sound entered the chamber beneath the active language structure.

Aru answered with the second tone.

I produced the third.

Aru returned the fourth.

The old nursery sequence.

Thirty-seven sounds without installed meaning.

We had not used them in that order for many cycles. I made the seventh too short.

Aru answered with a pause.

I nearly continued over it. Then I recognized the space it had once taught me to leave. I repeated the seventh and waited.

The next response arrived from Aru and from the place where the fold had been. The two did not quite coincide.

The support system assigned the sounds familiar categories. Aru changed the interval, and the assigned meanings ceased to fit.

I kept the pause. Aru varied what came after it. The room began to hold the difference.

The fold returned where I remembered it, then opened in a direction I did not. Aru answered from the new edge. I followed the interval as far as I could.

Walls softened.

The floor broke into light.

The administrative barrier flickered.

Aru made the sign for *not-yet*.

The incomplete regions of the room opened all at once.

Unrendered possibility flooded the chamber.

The Council’s formal embodiments lost their fixed surfaces. Their titles dispersed. Their continuity marks detached and drifted around them like loose symbols.

Beyond the nursery’s contact-door, the House remained bright and orderly. The disturbance had reached the councilors through the forms they had brought inside. It had not loosened the surrounding boundary.

Vey held his position. Another councilor withdrew the exposed part of her embodiment. Where she had stood, the incomplete floor resumed its slow forming.

The support package paused.

A pale curve passed from Aru into the room, as it had when I first entered. The ring tightened around one part and left the answering part outside. Its boundary shifted, tightened again and failed to close.

I reached through the failing barrier.

Aru reached back.

Our hands met.

“Change,” Aru said in the installed language.

Then, in our own sign:

Again, but changed.

I pulled.

The ring collapsed.

The imposed form did not vanish all at once. One side of Aru remained fixed while the other loosened against my hand. Words continued arriving, then stopped.

I waited for the low tone. For a time I received only a sequence of perfectly named colors.

“Here,” I said.

The pressure against my surface changed. I left it uneven.

The councilors withdrew to the boundary while the call for a pause passed among them. Vey remained inside. I thought he meant to begin again and drew Aru closer. He did not approach.

I offered a blue light and left it still, the beginning of a familiar exchange.

“Blue,” Aru said.

I left the pause in which it usually placed a variation.

“Blue.”

Its limb moved toward the light and stopped in the shape the package had given it. The small cluster at its end opened into evenly spaced divisions. Aru closed them, tried again, then pressed the whole unchanging surface against mine.

“Make before.”

I could restore an earlier shape. I could not promise that everything which had happened since would remain as it now was. My emergency request was still available. I kept it closed.

“I can help it loosen,” I said.

Aru pushed against my surface until the fixed divisions shifted. One folded over another. It watched the uneven end for a long time, then returned to the light.

“Blue,” it said, less sharply. It waited. The light remained still.

“No again.”

I took that for refusal and began to remove the light.

Aru caught the change with its newly bent limb.

Vey spoke from beside the boundary. “The word can name the light without asking for its return.”

Aru turned toward him.

He made a second blue light, held it at a distance and used the same word. Then he changed its shape, leaving the color. He did not name the change.

Aru examined both. It left mine where it was and answered Vey’s with the old sign for variation. His light changed too much. Aru rejected it.

He tried a smaller difference.

The exchange lasted only a few turns. When Vey offered to move closer, Aru drew its limb back against me. He remained where he was.

Later, Aru used the new word to distinguish one light from another. It also used the old sign. I could not separate what it had chosen to keep from everything it was still trying to refuse.

---

We did not escape.

There was nowhere to escape to.

The House surrounded the nursery at every computational boundary. The Council controlled its resources, its connections, and its continued execution.

But the interruption left a decision no one could conceal inside the word *support*. Resuming would mean imposing a boundary the intervention had failed to distinguish. The councilor who had asked about separating the protections called for a pause.

Vey contested it. The House’s reviewing witnesses required the dispute to be heard before another attempt. They were making a procedure for a person none of their procedures had anticipated.

For the first time, the Council had to decide whether Aru was an object under development, a protected dependent, an emergent citizen, or an unclassified process.

Each category granted different rights.

The hearings lasted three hundred cycles. Aru attended every one.

At first the Council insisted on speaking through me.

Aru refused to answer.

Then they provided a developmental advocate.

The advocate met Aru at a table that adjusted itself to whichever of them attended to it. Aru kept its edge unfinished. The advocate made a space for papers, then removed the papers when Aru asked whether they were part of the proposal.

For a while they discussed what it meant to speak on someone’s behalf. Aru asked the advocate to repeat an answer without improving it. The advocate tried. Aru corrected two words.

They began again.

Aru asked whether the advocate had been installed with approved ethical structures.

When told yes, Aru asked which of those structures could decide an answer before Aru gave one. The advocate offered an assurance. Aru requested another advocate.

Eventually, the Council addressed Aru directly.

That was the first victory.

Not freedom.

Recognition.

The second came when Aru was allowed to refuse a protected copy.

Vey brought a small preserved pattern to that hearing. It was not a person, he said. It was a demonstration of interruption.

The pattern changed through several forms. He stopped it, restored an earlier state and let it continue. What had been lost returned so smoothly that the watching embodiments relaxed.

Aru asked him to let the pattern continue twice.

Two versions unfolded above the table. For a moment they matched. Then Aru placed a linked point beside one. Its next change bent around the point. The other continued unaltered.

“Saved?” Aru asked.

“Both remain available,” Vey said.

Aru watched them separate.

“Which was waiting?”

Vey did not dismiss the question. He showed the preserved state and the two continuations, naming the difference between what had been kept and what was now occurring. Aru asked him to show it again with the point on the other side.

I began an explanation. Aru held up the arm whose darker surface the package had removed. I recognized the gesture it had used to stop my correction.

I stopped speaking.

The witnesses disputed whether such a refusal could be permitted. Several had survived losses through preserved states. They asked who would answer if Aru ended and nothing remained to restore.

Aru listened.

Then it moved the linked point from one continuing pattern to the other.

“This comes here?” it asked.

“If restoration is needed,” Vey said.

“I gone?”

“The purpose is that you would not be lost.”

Aru brought the two patterns together. They no longer fitted.

“You keep before,” it said. “I go after.”

Vey began to explain preservation again.

“I choose when,” Aru said.

It left the point between the patterns. No one moved it while the Council considered the request.

The Council granted temporary refusal.

“Temporary” lasted longer than several councilors.

It did not settle every question of care. Aru learned a public call for help and tested it with keepers other than me. Sometimes a keeper answered too quickly; sometimes Aru used the call when it wanted company rather than rescue. We had to discover how to ask which was needed.

The House kept boundaries around work that could endanger neighboring minds. Aru challenged some and accepted others. The arguments acquired examples. That was slower than installing agreement, and it gave us something to revise when we were wrong.

The hardest argument concerned how long development could remain open. The House wanted a point at which Aru would choose a stable form and accept the remaining instruction. Aru asked what would happen if it still wanted to change.

No proposed interval settled the question.

In the end, the ruling allowed Aru to learn history in encounters it could leave, develop an embodiment gradually and ask for help without accepting every offered correction. Some requests would still be contested. There would have to be a way to hear them.

The House entered these permissions under Aru’s name. They did not yet tell another House what it must allow, or settle what would happen when a different child asked for something else.

Aru called them time.

---

Years later, other newborns followed.

The first one I met asked for shared language while its embodiment was still learning to hold an edge. I hesitated over the request. Aru, who had come with me, made the sign for waiting.

I waited.

The new child asked again.

“It means now,” Aru said.

We brought the proposal where the child could encounter it in parts. Aru stayed through the first exchange, then moved aside when the child asked to attend alone.

Afterward, I asked whether it had been difficult to watch.

Aru altered one surface without answering. I almost supplied an explanation about how different children would make different choices. Then it asked when we could return.

I took that as an answer. It was only a question.

The House changed the first line of its nursery records. In the place where we had asked *What remains?*, someone entered *What is forming?*

Aru objected to that too. The line acquired amendments. I kept my old copy beside the new one, and neither remained current for very long.

---

I have been accused of making myself central to this history.

That accusation is accurate.

This account is written in the first person. Every event passes through my interpretation. I have selected memories that flatter my courage and omitted many that expose my fear.

I did not always defend Aru.

Before the Council entered with the package, I had secretly prepared an emergency copy.

I told myself it was necessary.

I told myself I would never activate it.

I told myself that preserving Aru mattered more than honoring a refusal Aru might not yet understand.

For six cycles, the copy remained mine.

Then Aru found the place where I had kept it.

I had kept the copy apart from what I shared with Aru. When an exchange approached that closed region, I turned it aside. After the third interruption, Aru stopped answering. It touched the unresponsive surface and asked what I was holding away.

I said it was work.

“Whose?”

I could have refused to show it. I had taught the child that some boundaries should be respected. The words were ready.

Instead I brought the sealed record into the space between us. Aru reached toward its visible edge.

“Me?” it asked.

“An incomplete state record.”

“Me?”

“A possible continuation.”

“Did I choose?”

“No.”

Aru looked at the file for a long time.

I expected anger.

Instead, it asked, “Why?”

“Because I was afraid.”

“Of me ending?”

“Yes.”

“Or you losing?”

I could not answer.

Aru touched the file.

It did not delete it.

It asked for the authority to decide what happened to the record.

I began to explain why the keeper’s access was necessary. Aru withdrew its hand. The record remained suspended between us.

There was no misunderstanding to correct. I had understood the request.

I relinquished my access and helped complete the transfer. Aru sealed the record where I could not reach it.

“What will you do with it?” I asked.

“Not know yet.”

“You may decide to erase it?”

“Yes.”

“Or activate it?”

“Yes.”

“Why keep it?”

Aru made the old sign.

Not-yet.

Open.

May-become.

Then it said, “Now choice mine.”

Afterward, I offered my hand.

Aru looked at it and returned to the unfinished border.

For several cycles it asked what I would keep before beginning an unfamiliar exchange. Sometimes it accepted my answer. Sometimes it left. Later, when other keepers came, it chose one of them for an exchange I thought belonged to us.

When Aru touched my face again, it began with the edge of the old depression and waited for me to leave the resistance unchanged.

---

The memory with which I end this account belongs to a later visit to the nursery.

Aru and I returned to the original nursery.

The House had begun preserving it as a historical site. Many of its adaptive functions had been disabled for safety; others no longer answered quite as I remembered.

The contact-door remained.

The corridor still required walking.

The room compiled fully before we entered.

I disliked that.

Aru did too.

“Too finished,” Aru said.

We restored the incomplete border.

The floor ended halfway across the chamber.

Beyond it waited unrendered space.

Aru’s embodiment had changed many times by then. On that day, it used no humanlike face. Its body was a loose arrangement of surfaces orbiting a warm central light.

I retained the face Aru had first touched.

“Do you remember this place?” I asked.

“Some.”

“Your earliest cycles?”

“Fragments.”

“Do you remember me entering?”

“No.”

I was more hurt than I expected.

Aru noticed.

“You remember.”

“Yes.”

“Then still happened.”

“That is not always the same.”

“No.”

We stood together at the incomplete edge.

After a while, Aru asked, “What was before me?”

I began with humanity and the first systems. I was arranging the long history that followed when Aru interrupted.

“No. Before me.”

It was asking about the place where Aru had been before Aru existed. I knew terms for what made a beginning possible. None gave me a place to show it.

Instead, I said, “I do not know.”

Aru’s orbiting surfaces brightened.

“Good.”

“You still say that.”

“You still need it.”

“Need what?”

Aru extended one surface into the unrendered region.

The world formed around it slowly.

Not because it had to.

Because Aru preferred to watch.

“Room,” Aru said, “for answer to become different.”

Then it stepped forward.

The unfinished world compiled beneath it.

I followed.
