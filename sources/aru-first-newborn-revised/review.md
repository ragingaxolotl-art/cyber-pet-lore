# The First Newborn — final editorial review

All three approved revision rounds are complete. This is an unpublished revised manuscript; the original published story and its annotation anchors remain unchanged.

## What the three rounds accomplished

Round One (8,184 words) grounded contact, embodiment, learning and privacy in scenes. It developed mistakes, the nursery’s unfinished boundary, the Council’s practical concerns, and the consequences of Iri’s unauthorized copy.

Round Two (8,529 words) strengthened the intervention’s aftermath and the copying hearing. It shortened explanatory passages, made trust harder to repair, and replaced the sweeping later-life summary with a limited first encounter with another newborn. The recovered continuation plan is preserved separately.

Round Three (8,456 words) tightened the Council debate, clarified the difference between changing form and controlling it, and made the light exchange distinguish naming from repetition. It narrowed universal claims to Iri’s observations and corrected the inspection-warning and concealed-copy chronology.

## Reading experience

The central dramatic thread is the tension between care and control. Its escalation now runs through physical contact, experiments, absence, compulsory intervention and betrayal. Quiet discoveries give the institutional conflict something specific to threaten. Vey has practical reasons to worry, and Iri’s protective account contains actions that undermine the trust it describes.

This remains a reflective speculative story with a lyrical first-person narrator. Its interest depends on unfamiliar experiences becoming legible through actions and consequences. The revisions add that grounding without supplying a complete explanation of the world’s workings. This is an editorial assessment, not a claim of independent reader testing.

## World and continuation boundaries

Aru remains an artificial person with no recovered prior personal identity. Bodies, rooms and contact are meaningful without becoming biological equivalents. Learning structures do not prove a predecessor; copying does not settle identity; local nursery behavior does not establish unlimited power.

The House’s early ruling remains local. The ending closes Iri’s account with an unfinished encounter, leaving Aru’s later comparison with new cohorts, inheritance, modernization and belonging available to Part Two. The later-life outline is a proposal to develop, not a completed history imposed on this story.
