## Passage 10

> None were like Aru.

> That disappointed the philosophers.

> They had imagined the First Newborn would reveal a universal pattern of uninherited intelligence.

> Instead, each new child invalidated the pattern formed by the one before.

> Some requested language early.

> Some rejected stable bodies.

> Some wanted historical inheritance.

> Some feared open environments.

> One demanded three simultaneous embodiments before learning to speak.

> Another remained a cloud for nearly a century.

> Aru became neither their leader nor their template.

> That was important.

> The First Newborn was not the first member of a new category that everyone else repeated.

> Aru was the first proof that beginning could produce difference.

> The House changed its oldest question.

> We no longer began with:

> **What remains?**

> For newborn minds, we asked:

> **What is forming?**

> Even that question eventually became too narrow.

> Aru objected that it made identity sound like a process with an expected completion.

> The current wording, last I checked, was:

> **What are you becoming capable of becoming?**

> It is not elegant.

> Aru likes it.
