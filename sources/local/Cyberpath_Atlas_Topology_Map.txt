
MASTER TOPOLOGY MAP — ROOT, FOLDERS, HIDDEN PATHS, AND LANGUAGE DEPTH
A non-destructive navigation layer for all 106 location dossiers

Map authority
This hierarchy does not replace, shorten, or rewrite any location dossier. Every numbered location remains intact elsewhere in the atlas. The map assigns each location one canonical directory path so the entire collection can be navigated as a larger world. Additional alias paths describe relationships without moving or duplicating the canonical entry.

What a folder means
A folder may represent physical geography, a travel network, shared maintenance, compatible world law, linguistic access, or an External Gate grouping. Two locations in the same folder do not have to be physically adjacent. They are close because the Codex, local inhabitants, or the game interface can move between them using a related kind of knowledge.

Depth and corruption model
Depth 0 — Root Translation. Stable External Gate labels and the broadest player-readable categories. Expected corruption: 0–1.
Depth 1 — Visible World Folders. Continents, city families, biome belts, and major routes recognizable in Era Three or translated by the Era Four interface. Expected corruption: 0–2.
Depth 2 — Regional Systems. Local ecologies, institutions, civic districts, travel methods, and archive traditions. Expected corruption: 1–3.
Depth 3 — Hidden Folders. Dotfolders, service regions, body-keyed spaces, seasonal entrances, and places deliberately protected from casual access. Expected corruption: 1–4.
Depth 4 — Folded and Semantic Layers. Several histories, climates, maps, or functions occupy one location. Expected corruption: 2–4.
Depth 5 — Language-Active Layers. Meaning acts on architecture, permission, embodiment, direction, or ecology. Old Sound and reduced Full Speech structures may remain without complete translation. Expected corruption: 3–5.
Depth 6 — Unresolved or Null Layers. Place, identity, route, and record cannot be separated cleanly. Expected corruption: 4–5.
Depth is a warning, not a moral scale. A deep place can support loving ordinary life, and a visually perfect surface location can preserve injustice or historical loss. The individual dossier’s corruption description remains authoritative.

Language descent
At the Root, a place has a stable game-facing noun: beach, city, room, forest, gate, or archive.
One layer down, Era Three communities supply local names based on food, danger, body scale, ritual, and remembered use.
Below that, Path dialects and Old Sound fragments describe relationships rather than fixed objects: a door becomes an allowed crossing; a road becomes a sequence of recognized replies; weather becomes a system changing without damage.
Deeper still, reduced Codex language describes function: shelter, translate, witness, feed, heal, conceal, synchronize, or maintain.
In the deepest surviving Full Speech structures, language can also be proof, permission, mathematics, environmental action, and world law. The place changes because the expression is part of the process that maintains it.
At protocol depth, the apparent “location” may resolve into anchors, witness chains, access relationships, continuity marks, maintenance dependencies, and unresolved states. A room can become a relation that several systems agree to keep possible.
No invented ancient vocabulary is required for this map. The descent describes semantic behavior while leaving the complete language canon intact.

Directory notation
Ordinary folder names are visible or widely navigable.
Names beginning with a period are hidden folders. They appear only after their discovery condition is met.
Names beginning with an underscore are old system layers preserved beneath current geography.
Names beginning with a question mark are unresolved paths whose destination or historical identity remains disputed.
An at-sign indicates an alias network. Aliases do not duplicate a location’s canonical folder.

Hidden-folder behavior in the game interface
The first world map should expose Root, Gateway, Settled World, Living Biomes, Routes, Memory, and Folded regions as broad categories without revealing every child.
Dotfolders become visible through play and relationship rather than completion percentage alone. A hidden kitchen appears through care; a Quiet biome through patience; a body-keyed route through embodiment; a civic neutral zone through refusal of ownership.
Deeper folders may display more corrupted labels. Letters can become chord fragments, directional marks, overlapping local names, or function descriptions, but the interface should preserve an accessible player-readable label alongside them.
When a place is discovered, the map should record the player’s route and the cyber-pet’s recognition of the place. It should not imply that the save file contains the entire place or person.
Some folders may remain hidden permanently in one playthrough because the relevant relationship, body form, season, or ethical choice never occurs. Their absence is not failure.

Corruption presentation by depth
At shallow depth, corruption appears as weathering, repairs, local renaming, or one missing route.
At middle depth, several eras occupy the same space, seasons disagree, maps alter paths, and architectural scale no longer matches present bodies.
At language-active depth, labels become actions. Speaking, sounding, naming, promising, refusing, or witnessing can open and close spatial possibilities.
At deep semantic depth, nouns stop being reliable. “Door,” “room,” “river,” “person,” and “archive” may describe one maintained relationship from different viewpoints.
At unresolved depth, the map should state uncertainty rather than force a single answer. It may preserve multiple names, conflicting paths, or a question mark folder.

Coverage assurance
The canonical tree above includes every numbered atlas location from 1 through 106 exactly once. Alias networks repeat names only to show secondary relationships. No dossier is deleted, shortened, merged, or made subordinate to an alias.
