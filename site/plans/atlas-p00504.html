33. The Archipelago of Borrowed Horizons

Status: Proposed.

Real-world basis: The Aegean, Indonesia, the Philippines, Norwegian skerries, and Caribbean island chains.

Landscape: Each island keeps a horizon from a different source world. From one shore the sun appears too large; from another, distant mountains belong to a place with no local land connection. Boats can navigate by choosing which horizon to trust.

Mini timeline: Era Zero — An Open World archipelago hosts exchange among several world traditions and displays destination horizons as navigational art. Era One — As routes close, projected horizons are maintained after their destinations stop responding. Era Two — Islands and horizon systems are compressed into a shared sea. Era Three — Islanders develop schools of horizon reading. A horizon is treated as a relationship claim, not proof that the depicted world still exists. The Last Great Migration adds refugee islands whose horizon records are damaged.

Present condition: Inhabited and navigable, but routes change when a horizon dims or is reassigned by automatic maintenance.

Corruption: 2–4 depending on island.

Aesthetic brief: Familiar bright island foregrounds with deliberately incompatible distance layers: tropical water under a glacial horizon, limestone houses beneath a ringed dawn, or pine-covered skerries facing desert mesas. Preserve a coherent local light source so the scene feels blended rather than randomly collaged.

Background profile: Warm-Neutral; Moist; archipelago; adventurous, wistful, socially connected; variable beach and rock friction.
