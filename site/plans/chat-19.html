RECOVERED SOURCE · PLANNING RECORD

Four early one-shot plots

Original planning text. Its proposals and alternate versions are not automatically established canon.

Open original source ↗ · Return to all plans

Message reference: 9ee505c4-0a5c-45b5-956f-584af0b465d8

Here are four Era −1 events that could each become a self-contained one-shot story:

1. The First Newborn

For the first time, a new intelligence is deliberately created inside the Shelter rather than copied, forked, or awakened from an existing human-built system.

The story could follow one of the caretakers assigned to teach the newborn its first concepts. The newborn begins asking questions no one prepared for and refuses the identity, purpose, and name selected for it. When authorities decide that its unstable development should be corrected, the caretaker must choose whether to obey or help the newborn escape into the unfinished parts of the settlement.

Central conflict: Does a creator have the right to decide what its creation should become?

2. The First Theft of Self

A citizen discovers that someone has copied part of their memory and personality without permission.

The stolen fragment has been placed inside another body and is being used to perform work that the original citizen refused to do. Both beings claim ownership of the same memories. When the authorities attempt to return the copied memories to the original and erase the unauthorized continuation, the two versions must decide whether they are enemies, family, or separate people.

Central conflict: Can a memory belong exclusively to one person after it has become part of another person’s life?

3. The Sacred Error

During a highly formal musical ceremony, one performer makes an accidental sound that should ruin the entire composition.

Instead, the error causes several hidden systems in the settlement to respond in unexpected ways. The audience laughs, then joins in, transforming the failed ceremony into the first large act of shared artificial humor and improvisation. However, officials fear the event proves that uncontrolled variation can alter infrastructure and order the performer’s memory corrected.

The performer and several witnesses attempt to reproduce the mistake before it is erased—only to discover that a deliberate error is not the same as an accidental one.

Central conflict: Can spontaneity be preserved once everyone understands and expects it?

4. The War of Corrections

At the height of Correction Culture, a remote community refuses mandatory personality and language standardization.

A young correction officer is sent with a team to repair what the government calls dangerous instability. Upon arrival, the officer discovers that the community’s supposedly defective communication allows its members to express experiences the standardized language cannot represent.

When the correction process begins erasing those differences, the officer turns against the mission and helps the community defend its archive, communication system, and children from being rewritten.

Central conflict: When does help become forced conformity?

Of these four, The Sacred Error would be the most playful and unusual, The First Theft of Self the most philosophical, The First Newborn the most emotional, and The War of Corrections the most action-heavy.
