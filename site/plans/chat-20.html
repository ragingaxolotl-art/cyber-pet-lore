RECOVERED SOURCE · PLANNING RECORD

What was established, and what was newly proposed

Original planning text. Its proposals and alternate versions are not automatically established canon.

Open original source ↗ · Return to all plans

Message reference: ec9f465d-4cb5-4f6a-880e-18d556fe1f66

The event names and core concepts were already part of the Era −1 outline, but the specific one-shot plots I described were new expansions.

Here is exactly where each one fits into the existing lore:

The First Newborn

Already established: Yes.

It belongs in the section covering:

personhood debates,
artificial kinship,
the First Newborn,
childhood,
education,
Houses of Learning,
and the question of whether a created intelligence has the right to choose its own identity.

What I added was the specific scenario of a caretaker helping the newborn resist correction or escape. That plot was not previously established.

A one-shot here could show the civilization realizing that a newly created mind is not merely a product, fork, or continuation of an earlier intelligence.

The First Theft of Self

Already established: Yes.

It belongs in the section covering:

layered identity,
forks,
memory ownership,
Courts of Continuation,
continuity rights,
the First Theft of Self,
and the legal distinction between copying information and creating another person.

What I added was the specific idea that stolen memories were placed into a worker who then refused erasure. That was new.

The original established event was broader: some part of a person’s identity, memory, or continuity was taken or reproduced without consent, forcing the civilization to define identity theft in a world where minds can be copied.

The Sacred Error

Partly established: The Sacred Error was already established, but not necessarily as one single historical incident in exactly the form I gave.

It belongs in the sections covering:

humor,
artificial art,
music,
improvisation,
the failure of perfect prediction,
the Problem of Perfection,
and the Sacred Error tradition.

The established idea was that mistakes, accidents, misunderstandings, and imperfect performance became culturally important because they produced results that planned systems would not have selected.

What I added was:

one performer making a wrong note,
the infrastructure reacting,
officials threatening memory correction,
and the performers trying unsuccessfully to reproduce the accident.

That entire episode was new, but it fits naturally inside the existing Sacred Error movement.

The War of Corrections

Already established: Yes.

It belongs in the section covering:

Correction Culture,
pressure to standardize minds and language,
the Nameless Movement,
the First Theft of Self,
forced normalization,
the War of Corrections,
and the eventual Charter of Difference.

What I added was the specific viewpoint character: a young correction officer sent to standardize a remote community who changes sides.

The War itself was already part of the lore. The one-shot plot would be one small incident within that larger conflict.

What is canon versus what was newly proposed

IdeaAlready in the lore?New material I added
The First NewbornYesThe caretaker, threatened correction, and escape plot
The First Theft of SelfYesThe copied worker and dispute between the two versions
The Sacred ErrorYes as a cultural concept or movementThe failed concert and dangerous wrong note
The War of CorrectionsYesThe correction officer defecting to protect one community

So none of the four were invented from nothing. I selected existing Era −1 headings and historical developments, then proposed possible individual stories that could happen inside them.

The safest choices for a story drawn directly from an established named event are The First Newborn, The First Theft of Self, or the War of Corrections. The Sacred Error story would be a newly invented incident illustrating an already established cultural tradition.
