46. The Redwood Rain Vault

Status: Proposed.

Real-world basis: California redwood forests, Pacific Northwest temperate rainforest, Tasmanian giant forests, and Yakushima cedar groves.

Landscape: Immense trees grow around a Quiet World memory vault. Their rings and fungal networks regulate when stored material can influence the living forest.

Mini timeline: Era Zero — The vault is designed so memory enters current life slowly, through ecological exchange rather than direct download. Era One — Scholars install faster access ports and disturb the fungal gatekeeping network. Era Two — Stewards remove most ports and preserve the trees, fog cycle, and root exchange. Era Three — Little peoples live in fallen-log Nests and read safe archive seasons through mushroom blooms. One district remains unusually pristine because its access was never accelerated.

Present condition: Among the best-preserved living locations in the atlas, though several artificial access scars remain.

Corruption: 0–2.

Aesthetic brief: Giant red trunks, fern floor, fog shafts, rain beads, luminous mycelium, and tiny dwellings emphasizing scale. Minor corruption appears as rectangular wounds in bark where old access ports healed imperfectly.

Background profile: Cool; Humid; temperate rainforest/archive; calm, ancient, nurturing; soft forest-floor friction. Cozy sheltered zones may support rest recovery.
