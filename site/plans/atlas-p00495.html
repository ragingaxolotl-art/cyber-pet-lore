32. The Inland Sea of Doors

Status: Proposed.

Real-world basis: The Aral Sea’s exposed seabed, the Dead Sea, Great Lakes shorelines, drowned towns, and monumental flood-control structures.

Landscape: A retreating inland sea reveals hundreds of enormous doorframes standing in salt mud. They were once transfer thresholds between shoreline habitats, not ordinary doors. Some still open onto water conditions that no longer exist locally.

Mini timeline: Era Zero — A network of lake worlds shares seasonal water, migrating life, and body-compatible entry habitats. Era One — Optimization keeps water levels stable for too long, weakening local adaptation. Era Two — Several lake environments are folded into one basin; thresholds remain because they carry emergency routes. Era Three — The sea retreats during the Dimming. Settlements form around doorframes that occasionally release fog, fish, rain, or a current from a vanished shore. Wars occur over the most reliable thresholds before Pathkeepers establish shared access seasons.

Present condition: Broad salt flats, surviving deep water, and active but unpredictable thresholds.

Corruption: 4 — Multiple lost shore systems overlaid in one basin.

Aesthetic brief: White salt shore, shallow mirror water, rust-red reeds, and giant freestanding frames receding to the horizon. Each open frame shows a subtly different sky or water color. Major corruption should be localized: a doorway releases a sideways tide or opens into a vertical lake.

Background profile: Warm by day, Cold at night; Bonedry shore with Moist door zones; salt-lake/ruin; lonely, monumental, contested; firm salt friction with deep mud pockets.
