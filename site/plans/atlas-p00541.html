36. The Marble Dune Cemetery

Status: Proposed.

Real-world basis: Egypt’s White Desert, Lençóis Maranhenses, ancient necropolises, and eroded marble quarries.

Landscape: Pale dunes flow through rows of abstract stone forms. They are not graves in a human sense. Each marker denotes a world-law, body affordance, or ecological relation intentionally allowed to end during the Great Reduction.

Mini timeline: Era Two — Reduction councils create markers so eliminated possibilities will not be mistaken for failures never considered. The site records decisions without keeping every lost system active. Era Three — Wind exposes and covers different markers. Local peoples call it a cemetery because the old distinction between ended law, ended person, and ended habitat is no longer fully available. Some Num Seekers excavate the dunes hoping that a named loss can be restored.

Present condition: Mostly intact as a memorial, frequently misread as a storehouse of dormant powers.

Corruption: 2–3 — The markers remain; category knowledge has degraded.

Aesthetic brief: Cream and white dunes, sculpted stone forms resembling incomplete arches or bodies without faces, and blue shadows. No horror imagery. Major-corruption area: two incompatible markers have fused and generate a small zone with contradictory gravity.

Background profile: Hot; Dry; memorial desert; quiet, reverent, tempting; low sand friction.

Why the cemetery is surreal

The site resembles a cemetery only because later peoples no longer retain the full distinction between an ended person, an ended habitat, and an intentionally ended world-law. Its abstract markers do not represent human graves. They record body affordances, ecological relations, and possibilities that Reduction councils allowed to end while preserving proof that those losses had been considered.

Wind continually changes which decisions can be seen, turning the memorial into a shifting catalog. The most corrupted area makes the record physical: two incompatible markers have fused, creating a small zone where gravity contradicts itself. The result is quiet and reverent rather than horrific, but it is tempting to mistake remembrance for dormant power.
