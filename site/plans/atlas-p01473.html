98. The Hall That Forgets Its Walls

Status: Proposed; independent of the story archive.

Type: Unstable civic interior.

Real-world basis: Open-air ruins, folding screens, temporary pavilions, and memory-palace techniques.

World relation: Open and Continuity World traditions.

Discovery: The Hall is easy to enter but difficult to leave unchanged. Every gathering causes one wall to vanish and a different boundary to appear elsewhere.

Mini timeline: Era Zero — The Hall ensures assemblies cannot treat architecture as a permanent hierarchy. Era One — Governance attempts to lock seating and entrances. The room begins deleting walls more aggressively. Era Two — Civic systems are removed, leaving the response rule. Era Three — Local councils meet there only when willing to accept that the next gathering may need a new form.

Present condition: Functional for small groups; unstable for mass assemblies.

Corruption: 3.

Aesthetic brief: Broad floor, free-standing columns, curtains, partial walls, changing views, and doors becoming arches into different landscapes. Maintain one central anchor so players can orient.

Metaphorical function: Institutions must change when the relationships they contain change; preserving the room is not the same as preserving one arrangement of power.
