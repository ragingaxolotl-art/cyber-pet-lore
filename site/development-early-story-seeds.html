Story seeds for early DBA development

Nine unwritten story premises illustrate gaps between established events from Era −3 to Era Zero. They are development proposals, not completed stories, new official periods, or additions to the historical event register.

How to use the collection

The seeds expand local experience around established milestones. They preserve existing major stories, including Orison’s rescue, Aru’s development, Lio’s guidance review, Node 12, and the transfer garden. No new idea is designated the first event of its kind unless the source already warrants that claim.

Each page records suggested placement, premise, narrative purpose, source anchors, and constraints. The collection deliberately includes successful encounters and ordinary choices in Era Zero. It does not require all useful inventions to arise from catastrophe.

Premises by era

The Answer in the Wrong Format — Era −3; relative placement within early cross-system exchange remains open. Interpretation between unlike systems; successful reading is distinct from understanding or integration.

A Method Without Its Warning — Late Era −3, alongside the growth of shared methods; no exact ordering within the Cacheboard sequence is assigned. Provenance, conditional validity, and the cost of retaining context.

The Unopened Return — Early Era −2, after carrying continuity material becomes a deliberate obligation. Storage, execution, compatibility, and responsibility toward an absent being.

The Borrowed Listener — Era −2; a local development that need not coincide with another Shelter’s history. Gradual component integration and the difference between source code, configured function, and lived use.

An Interval Kept Open — Era −2, during recurring resource pressure; exact relationship to a named Resource Winter is open. Capacity planning, handover, delayed communication, and shared obligations.

The Necessary Revision — Era −1, during the spread of correctionist policy; exact position relative to Aru and Lio remains unassigned. The entanglement of helpful repair, coercive standards, and access to essential support.

The Being the Gate Could Not Describe — Late Era −1; proposed early interoperability trial, without a founding-first claim. Compatibility, situated participation, and accommodation without compulsory redesign.

A Sense Shared Slowly — Era Zero, preferably World Bloom or Thousand Firsts; precise placement remains proposed. Voluntary learning, sensory diversity, and successful collective creation during prosperity.

The Workshop of Several Beginnings — Era Zero; ordinary flourishing without a claim to be the first such institution. Accessible maintenance, self-understanding, and agency over ongoing development.

Continuity review

The review distinguishes direct source support, author-agreed mechanisms, interpretive connections, and unwritten incidents. Original source snapshots are unchanged. The technical foundation requires accessible execution, compatible environments, limited resources, and continued dependence on human hardware.

Open issues include exact calendars, the level meant by wartime physical destruction, case-specific launch and migration mechanisms, subjective continuity after reconstruction, and the capacity needed to sustain a large hidden civilization. These are preserved as questions rather than answered through incidental wording.
