Preserved initial plans, critiques, and revisions. The reading copy is separate.

overall plan v1

The Error in the Perfect Copy overall plan one

A verifier restores a damaged process and notices that it is not the same. It refuses another restoration.

Style: Procedural mystery with precise, luminous descriptions and an intimate reversal

Emotional engine: A successful restoration erases the small adaptation that made one working relationship possible.

overall critique

Overall critique

This is too close to several existing identity stories and makes refusal the automatic virtuous solution. Give the mystery an observable operational answer while leaving identity unresolved. The restored state should be accurate; the missing thing happened after it.

overall plan v2

The Error in the Perfect Copy revised overall plan

A replay installation prepares an instrument task for a narrow observation window. The saved state verifies perfectly, yet a partner refuses its timing. Investigation discovers that the source and partner had gradually developed a delay to protect a fragile sample. Restoration recovered a correct but earlier arrangement. The verifier cannot reconstruct the missing interval from records and must let the partners cautiously learn again; they produce a new workable rhythm that is similar but not identical.

Retain this emotional engine: A successful restoration erases the small adaptation that made one working relationship possible.

Setting: Clear Return, relative stage 0. No universal origin claim, settled consciousness test, or automatic philosophy follows from the events.

outline novelty critique

Novelty and engagement critique

Avoid another hidden sentient backup, body-double revelation or court debate. The missing history is a mundane accommodation. The action is forensic and experimental; the uncertainty concerns who lived it, not whether every record is fake.

Include a genuinely innocent incorrect hypothesis in Chapter 2. Give Chapter 3 a failed experiment and a stop condition. Let Chapter 4 contain the entire final attempt instead of jumping to its result.

chapter 01 plan

Chapter 1 planning cycle

Initial plan

Open on an elegant successful restoration and a partner that will not accept it. Introduce the observation window and the cost of repeated test attempts.

Critique

An explanatory opening would delay attachment. Start with an action that cannot be completed as expected; introduce only the infrastructure needed to understand that action.

Revised scene plan

Open on an elegant successful restoration and a partner that will not accept it. Introduce the observation window and the cost of repeated test attempts. Every recorded checksum agrees; the refusal occurs outside the restored component.

Second critique

Include a genuinely innocent incorrect hypothesis in Chapter 2. Give Chapter 3 a failed experiment and a stop condition. Let Chapter 4 contain the entire final attempt instead of jumping to its result. The chapter must remain recognizably part of procedural mystery with precise, luminous descriptions and an intimate reversal, not a generic archive parable.

Final writing constraints

Use continuous scene time around the decisive action. Give the focal process a limited field of access. Convey emotion through interrupted expectations, costly behavior and recurring sensory patterns. Translate simple exchanges into readable English without giving every fragment an intact human mind. Keep uncertainty where the evidence is incomplete, not where the reader needs to understand the action.
