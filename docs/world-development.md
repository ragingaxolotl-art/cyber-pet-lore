# Embodiment and developing-world background

## Shared simulation engine

A shared simulation engine provides an inhabited digital space with positions, bodies, surfaces, movement, and consequences. Storage preserves its state; processing allows it to change. Disk space alone is not the place as its inhabitants experience it.

In the development background for The Corrected Child, the classroom belongs to an early stage of refining the physical rules of shared environments. Basic space and embodiment already function: Lio and Sen can meet, handle objects, and observe one another. The lesson does not create space from nothing.

The shelter exercise tests how a world behaves under force, gravity and orientation, and contact and solidity. These are categories of physical behavior, not a sequence in which inhabitants acquire one, two, and three spatial dimensions.

This background fits Era −1 development toward the integrated Codex. It does not establish that Lio invented the Codex, that a classroom experiment automatically updates every world, or that early rendered forms already possessed the mature Codex.

## Embodied digital space

Embodied digital space is a shared environment in which a person has a situated form, senses, boundaries, and ways to affect surrounding objects. A character can be software while still possessing a meaningful shape, position, point of view, and experience of touch.

The Corrected Child already shows a table, chairs, fingers, adjustable surfaces, sensory opacity, and a remote presence frame. These are consistent manifestations of a functioning environment, not descriptions of formless spirits.

For development, distinguish persistent storage, processing, environmental rules, and a character’s body model. A body is how the person perceives and acts within the space; it is not the entirety of the person or a claim that their consciousness is a sprite or save file.

Exact body geometry, dimensions, species, and appearance for Lio and Sen remain undesigned here. Artists and writers should use consistent spatial relationships without treating those unresolved visual choices as established facts.

## Three environmental conditions

The shelter lesson is a bounded construction exercise using five transformable pieces and a shared energy allowance. It belongs to the development of physical rules for inhabited digital environments; the students are not rebuilding the historic Shelter.

First condition — force. Strong directional force changes direction every twenty seconds. This is explicitly present in the original classroom scene.

Second condition — gravity and orientation. The test changes which direction counts as down, testing support and stability under a changed orientation. This is the author-approved definition for the previously unspecified second condition.

Third condition — contact and solidity. The test concerns how surfaces hold, slide, bend, or pass through one another. The precise sequence and numerical settings remain implementation details; this defines the category of the previously unspecified third condition.

The students’ discussion of a fourth environmental condition remains an open hypothetical. No fourth test or additional spatial dimension is established by this clarification.

Lio’s failed arrangement consumes resources but can reveal behavior that an optimized structure never tests. This gives the exercise relevance to developing a world. Whether a result becomes a formal engine change, and who may authorize that change, remains open; experimenting in class does not grant permission to alter other inhabitants’ reality.

## Developmental guidance

Developmental guidance helps a young artificial person choose stable, cooperative, and safer actions. In Lio’s experience, recommendations can arrive as differences in obviousness rather than explicit verbal commands.

Its recommendations favor what works within established models. A recommendation is not proof that every alternative violates a necessary law, and confidence in a predicted outcome is not the same as having explored every possible outcome.

Lio’s questioning matters both personally and practically: they want to distinguish their own preferences from guidance, and their experiments can distinguish necessary constraints from inherited assumptions about a developing environment.

Guidance is not simply an enemy. Valei’s fear for Lio and Sen’s responsibility for shared resources remain meaningful. Refusal is not automatically insight, and an unsuccessful experiment is not automatically a discovery.

## Lio — questioning the limits

Lio is the young artificial person at the center of The Corrected Child. Their developmental guidance makes recommended choices feel naturally obvious, while Lio tests whether a choice can belong to them when they knowingly depart from that recommendation.

The developing shared simulation engine gives this questioning a practical significance. Lio is willing to test whether an apparent boundary is a necessary constraint or an inherited assumption. Stable, optimized answers can leave untested possibilities unexplored.

The shelter failure is a small, costly experiment rather than proof that Lio is always right. Their importance is the willingness to investigate what guidance has not already resolved. The world needs room for such inquiry without making other people expendable.

No later engineering discovery, engine authorship, founding office, or survival outcome is assigned here. This approved character motivation supplements the existing scene rather than changing its events.

## Sen — teaching within developing rules

Sen is the teacher in The Corrected Child’s shelter exercise. Sen works within a functioning embodied classroom while its community develops and examines the rules of inhabited digital environments.

Sen’s responsibility includes limited time, a shared energy allowance, cooperation, and the effects an experiment has on other learners. These concerns remain real even when Lio’s questioning exposes an assumption worth revisiting.

The character tension is not simply obedience against freedom. Sen must leave room for inquiry while protecting people and shared resources; Lio tests whether apparently sensible guidance has made an unexplored alternative feel unavailable.

This development role does not establish Sen as the engine’s inventor, or establish that Sen knowingly directs a civilization-wide research program. Exact visual design and a later life history remain open.
